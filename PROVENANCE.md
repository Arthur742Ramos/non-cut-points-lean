# Sources and scope

The mathematical source is Paul Bankston, *Metric Topology: A First Course*, spring 2011, [Lecture 29, Propositions 29.1 and 29.3](https://www.mscsnet.mu.edu/~paul/Paper/4450102text.pdf#page=91), printed pages 91 through 93. The relevant propositions and their proofs were read for this task. Proposition 29.3 states both the existence of two non-cut points and irreducibility about the set of non-cut points for nondegenerate Hausdorff continua. No permission or endorsement from the source author is claimed.

The local union helper proves the singleton case needed from Proposition 29.1. It does not assume that an open side of a cut is connected. The nesting argument retains the source's common-anchor reasoning. The final Zorn argument uses reverse inclusion on oriented compact tails: a point in a chain intersection produces a tail below every chain member. This replaces the exposition's maximal-family argument. No connectedness theorem for intersections is needed.

The first selected theorem uses arbitrary sets in an ambient Hausdorff space and assumes compactness, connectedness and nontriviality only for `C`. It asserts connectedness after each of two individual removals. The second selected theorem uses the explicit predicate `x ∈ C ∧ IsConnected (C \ {x})` and asserts equality for a compact connected subset containing all such points. It also permits singleton `C`; its proof only requires connectedness of `K`, though compactness of `K` is retained in the selected subcontinuum statement. The predicate counts an empty removal as disconnected under Mathlib's nonempty `IsConnected` convention; nontriviality in the first theorem excludes that case.
